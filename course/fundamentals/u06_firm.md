# F6 · Revenue, cost and profit | Reasoning

## F6.1 Revenue
**Revenue** (sales, turnover) = price × quantity sold, summed over products:

```
R = Σ Pᵢ × Qᵢ
```

Revenue is **not** profit: it's the money coming in before any cost.
Average revenue per unit = R / Q; with one price, it equals the price.

> **Example.** 300 phones at €250 and 100 tablets at €320 → R = 75,000 +
> 32,000 = **€107,000**; average revenue per unit = 107,000/400 = €267.50.

Growth decomposition: %ΔR ≈ %ΔP + %ΔQ.

@num 120 units at €45 and 80 units at €60. Total revenue (€)?
= 10200
! 5,400 + 4,800.
@num Same sales: average revenue per unit (€)?
= 51
! 10,200/200.
@num Price +4 %, quantity +6 %. Approximate revenue growth (%)?
= 10 % ~0.3
! ≈ 4 + 6 (exact 1.04 × 1.06 = 1.1024).

## F6.2 Fixed and variable costs
- **Fixed costs (FC)** don't change with output in the period: rent,
  salaries, insurance, software licences.
- **Variable costs (VC)** change with output: materials, components, energy
  per unit, shipping per unit.

```
total cost TC = FC + VC = FC + v × Q       (v = variable cost per unit)
```

Whether a cost is fixed depends on the horizon: in the long run all costs can
change.

> **Example.** FC = €4,000/month, v = €15 → 300 units cost 4,000 + 4,500 =
> €8,500.

@num FC €6,000, variable cost €12 per unit. Total cost of 500 units (€)?
= 12000
! 6,000 + 6,000.
@mcq Which is a variable cost for a phone maker?
+ chips used in each phone
- factory rent
- the CEO's salary
- annual software licence
! Rises with each unit produced.
@num Total cost of 200 units is €9,000 and of 300 units is €11,500. Variable cost per unit (€)?
= 25
! 2,500/100.

## F6.3 Average and marginal cost
```
average cost  AC = TC / Q
marginal cost MC = ΔTC / ΔQ      (cost of one more unit)
```

With constant variable cost v and fixed cost FC: AC = FC/Q + v (falls as
fixed costs are spread), MC = v (constant).

Decisions about **one more unit** use MC, not AC (F1.6).

> **Example.** FC 10,000, v 20. At Q = 500: AC = 20 + 20 = 40. The 501st unit
> costs only 20 extra.

@num FC €8,000, v = €30. Average cost at Q = 400 (€)?
= 50
! 8,000/400 + 30.
@num Same firm: marginal cost of the 401st unit (€)?
= 30
x 50 | marginal cost excludes fixed costs already paid.
! Only the variable cost.
@num TC(100) = 5,000; TC(101) = 5,038. Marginal cost of unit 101?
= 38
! 5,038 − 5,000.

## F6.4 Profit
```
profit = revenue − total cost = (P − v) × Q − FC
```

Profit per unit sold = P − AC. A firm can have high revenue and a loss (costs
higher), or low revenue and a profit.

> **Example.** P = 50, v = 30, FC = 8,000, Q = 500 → profit = 20 × 500 −
> 8,000 = **€2,000**.

Distinguish **gross profit** (revenue − cost of goods sold), **operating
profit** (also minus overheads) and **net profit** (after interest and tax)
— unit F10.

@num P = €80, v = €50, FC = €12,000, Q = 600. Profit (€)?
= 6000
! 30 × 600 − 12,000.
@num Same firm at Q = 300. Profit (€)?
= -3000
! 9,000 − 12,000.
@num Revenue €250,000, total costs €232,000. Profit as % of revenue?
= 7.2 %
! 18,000/250,000.

## F6.5 Markup and margin
For a unit with cost C and price P:

```
markup (on cost)  = (P − C) / C
margin (on price) = (P − C) / P
margin = markup / (1 + markup)        markup = margin / (1 − margin)
```

> **Example.** C = 60, P = 90 → markup 50 %, margin 33.3 %.

Confusing the two is a classic, expensive mistake: a "40 % margin" on a €60
cost needs P = 60/(1 − 0.4) = **€100**, not 60 × 1.4 = €84.

@num Cost €40, price €50. Markup on cost (%)?
= 25 %
x 20 | that's the margin on price.
! 10/40.
@num Cost €40, price €50. Margin on price (%)?
= 20 %
x 25 | that's the markup on cost.
! 10/50.
@num Cost €70. Price that gives a 30 % margin on price (€)?
= 100
x 91 | a 30 % MARGIN is on price: P = C/(1 − 0.3).
! 70/0.7.
@num A 60 % markup corresponds to which margin (%)?
= 37.5 %
! 0.6/1.6.

## F6.6 Contribution margin and break-even
**Contribution margin** per unit = P − v: what each unit contributes towards
fixed costs and then profit.

```
break-even quantity Q_BE = FC / (P − v)
units for target profit  = (FC + target) / (P − v)
```

> **Example.** FC 24,000, P 50, v 20 → CM 30 → Q_BE = 800 units.

**Margin of safety** = (expected sales − break-even)/expected sales: how far
sales can fall before the firm starts losing money. Below break-even every unit
still contributes, but not enough to cover the fixed costs.

@num FC €36,000, P €60, v €36. Break-even quantity?
= 1500
! 36,000/24.
@num Same firm: units needed for €12,000 profit?
= 2000
! 48,000/24.
@num Expected sales 2,000, break-even 1,500. Margin of safety (%)?
= 25 %
! 500/2,000.

## F6.7 Economies of scale
**Economies of scale**: average cost falls as output grows — spreading fixed
costs, bulk discounts, specialised machines and staff. **Diseconomies**: AC
rises (coordination, congestion, overtime).

With FC and constant v, AC = FC/Q + v always falls but flattens: the first
increases in volume save a lot, later ones little.

> **Example.** FC 50,000, v 10: AC = 110 at Q = 500, 60 at 1,000, 35 at 2,000,
> 22.5 at 4,000.

@num FC €50,000, v €10. Average cost at Q = 2,500 (€)?
= 30
! 20 + 10.
@num Same firm: by how many € does AC fall from Q = 1,000 to Q = 2,000?
= 25
! 60 − 35.
@mcq Bulk discounts on inputs for large orders are a source of…
+ economies of scale
- diseconomies of scale
- sunk costs
- price discrimination by the buyer
! Lower unit cost at larger scale.

## F6.8 Unit economics
**Unit economics** analyses profit per unit (or per customer/order) before
fixed costs: price, variable costs, contribution. A business with negative
contribution per unit **loses more as it grows**.

Typical decomposition for one order:

```
price − materials − shipping − payment fees − returns allowance = contribution
```

> **Example.** Price 40; materials 18; shipping 6; fees 1.20 (3 % of price);
> returns allowance 2 → contribution €12.80 per order.

@num Price €60; materials €25; shipping €8; payment fee 2 % of price; returns allowance €3. Contribution per order (€)?
= 22.8
! 60 − 25 − 8 − 1.2 − 3.
@num Same business: fixed costs €45,600 per month. Orders needed to break even?
= 2000
! 45,600/22.8.
@mcq A start-up with negative contribution per unit…
+ loses more money the more it sells
- becomes profitable with scale | fixed-cost spreading can't fix a negative unit margin.
- has no fixed costs
- should cut prices
! Each extra unit adds a loss.

## F6.9 Pricing basics
Three ways firms set prices:

- **Cost-plus**: price = cost × (1 + markup). Simple, ignores customers.
- **Value-based**: price from what customers are willing to pay.
- **Competition-based**: relative to rivals' prices.

Economics adds: the profit-maximising price depends on **both cost and how
quantity reacts to price** (elasticity). A higher price raises margin per unit
but sells fewer units; the best price balances the two.

> **Example.** Cost 20. At 40 you sell 100 (profit 2,000); at 50 you sell 70
> (profit 2,100); at 60 you sell 40 (profit 1,600). Best of the three: 50.

@num Cost €10 per unit. At €25 you sell 200; at €30 you sell 150; at €35 you sell 90. Highest profit (€)?
= 3000
! 15×200 = 3,000; 20×150 = 3,000; 25×90 = 2,250 → 3,000.
@num Cost €8; at €20 you sell 300, at €24 you sell 240. Profit difference (24 minus 20, €)?
= 240
! 16×240 − 12×300 = 3,840 − 3,600.
@mcq Cost-plus pricing ignores…
+ how much customers are willing to pay and how quantity reacts
- costs
- the markup
- arithmetic
! It looks only at the cost side.

## F6.10 Capacity and scarce inputs
When output is limited by a **capacity** or a **scarce input**, the question
changes from "is this unit profitable?" to "**which** units are most
profitable per unit of the scarce resource?"

```
rank by: contribution per unit of the scarce resource
```

> **Example.** 100 machine-hours. Product X earns €30 and uses 3 h (€10/h);
> product Y earns €20 and uses 1 h (€20/h). Making Y first earns €2,000 in
> 100 h vs €1,000 with X only.

(Developed further in F11.)

@num A scarce input: 60 units available. Product A earns €40 and uses 4 units; product B earns €15 and uses 1 unit. Maximum contribution with unlimited demand (€)?
= 900
x 600 | rank by contribution per unit of the scarce input: B earns 15 per unit, A 10.
! B: 60 × 15 = 900.
@num Same, but demand for B is at most 20 units. Maximum contribution (€)?
= 700
! 20 B (20 inputs, €300) + 10 A (40 inputs, €400).
@mcq With a scarce input, products should be ranked by…
+ contribution per unit of the scarce input
- contribution per unit of product
- price
- revenue
! The constraint is the scarce input.

## F6.11 Cash is not profit
Profit measures value created in a period; **cash** measures money in the
bank. They differ because of **timing**:

- You may buy materials (cash out) long before selling the products.
- Customers may pay later (sales recorded, no cash yet).
- Unsold stock is cash turned into goods — not a cost in profit until sold,
  but the cash is gone.

> **Example.** A firm buys €10,000 of components, makes goods, sells half for
> €12,000 cash. Profit on goods sold: 12,000 − 5,000 = €7,000. Cash change:
> −10,000 + 12,000 = **+€2,000**. €5,000 sits in unsold stock.

Many profitable firms fail because they run out of cash.

@num Buy materials €8,000 (paid), sell 60 % of the resulting goods for €9,000 (paid). Change in cash (€)?
= 1000
! −8,000 + 9,000.
@num Same: profit on the goods sold (€)?
= 4200
! 9,000 − 0.6 × 8,000.
@num Same: value of materials still sitting in stock (€)?
= 3200
! 0.4 × 8,000.

## F6.12 Break-even with several products
With several products, use the **weighted average contribution margin**
(weights = sales mix):

```
weighted CM = Σ (share_i × CM_i)
break-even units (total) = FC / weighted CM
```

> **Example.** Mix 75 % A (CM 10), 25 % B (CM 30): weighted CM = 15. FC 30,000
> → 2,000 units in total: 1,500 A and 500 B.

If the mix shifts towards low-CM products, break-even rises — even when total
units sold stay the same, profit can fall because the *mix* changed.

@num Mix 60 % A (CM €20) and 40 % B (CM €45). Weighted contribution margin (€)?
= 30
! 12 + 18.
@num Same mix, fixed costs €48,000. Total break-even units?
= 1600
! 48,000/30.
@num Same: how many of those are B?
= 640
! 0.4 × 1,600.
