# 3.2 The income statement: revenue, costs, margins

## The big picture

The **income statement** (P&L, profit and loss, *Conto Economico*) measures
performance over a period: revenues earned minus expenses incurred = profit.
Profit is **not** cash (lesson 3.3) — understanding the difference is one of
the most valuable skills in business.

> **Analogy.** The P&L is a match report counting goals scored (revenue) and
> conceded (costs). The cash statement is the team's bank account — a team can
> win the season and still run out of money for salaries.

## Structure

```
  Revenue (sales)
− Cost of goods sold (COGS)
= Gross profit                          gross margin = GP / revenue
− Operating expenses (SG&A, R&D, depreciation)
= Operating profit (EBIT)               operating margin = EBIT / revenue
− Interest
= Profit before tax (EBT)
− Taxes
= Net income                            net margin = NI / revenue
```

**EBITDA** = EBIT + depreciation & amortisation — a rough proxy for operating
cash generation.

## Accrual accounting

- **Revenue recognition**: when the good is delivered / the obligation is
  satisfied, not when cash arrives.
- **Matching principle**: expenses are recorded in the same period as the
  revenues they help generate. **COGS** counts only the cost of the units
  **sold** this period, not of everything bought.

*Worked example.* In a month a firm buys materials for €5,000, turns them into
goods, sells 60 % of those goods for €9,000 (customers pay next month).
COGS = 0.6 × 5,000 = 3,000. Gross profit = 9,000 − 3,000 = **€6,000**.
Cash flow this month = −5,000 (purchases) + 0 (customers haven't paid) =
**−€5,000**. Profitable, yet cash went down. The remaining €2,000 of goods sit
in inventory on the balance sheet.

## Margins

- **Gross margin** = (revenue − COGS)/revenue: how much each euro of sales
  leaves after direct costs.
- **Markup** = (price − cost)/cost. Do not confuse with margin:
  price 150, cost 100 → markup 50 %, margin 33.3 %.
  margin = markup/(1 + markup); markup = margin/(1 − margin).
- **Contribution margin** (lesson 3.4) = price − variable cost per unit.

*Worked example.* Price €80, unit cost €50 → markup 60 %, margin 37.5 %.

## Profitability ratios

- **ROS** (return on sales) = EBIT/revenue.
- **ROA** = EBIT/total assets (or net income/total assets).
- **ROE** = net income/equity.
- **DuPont decomposition**:
  ROE = (NI/Sales) × (Sales/Assets) × (Assets/Equity) = margin × turnover ×
  leverage. Two firms with the same ROE can be a high-margin jeweller and a
  low-margin, high-turnover discounter.

## Revenue drivers

Revenue = Σ price × quantity across products. Growth comes from more units,
higher prices, or a better **mix** (selling more high-priced products).
**Revenue per unit of a constrained resource** (per hour of machine time, per
square metre of shelf, per unit of a scarce input) is the key comparison when
a resource limits output (lesson 3.4).

## Summary

- P&L: revenue − COGS = gross profit; − opex = EBIT; − interest, tax = net.
- Accrual accounting records when earned/incurred, not when paid.
- Markup on cost vs margin on price; know how to convert.
- DuPont: ROE = margin × asset turnover × leverage.
