# 1.7 Perfect competition

## The big picture

Market structure describes how many firms compete and how similar their
products are. At one extreme is **perfect competition**: many small firms,
identical products, free entry, full information. No firm can influence the
price — each is a **price taker**. It is a benchmark: few markets fit it
exactly, but it shows what competition does to prices and profits.

> **Analogy.** A single tomato grower at a huge wholesale market is like one
> drop in the sea: if she asks 1 cent above the market price, nobody buys
> from her; she can sell all she wants at the market price.

## The price-taking firm

For a price taker, marginal revenue (MR) equals the price P: each extra unit
sells for P. Profit is maximised where

```
P = MC     (with MC rising)
```

Profit = (P − ATC) · Q.

*Worked example.* TC = 100 + 4Q + Q² (lesson 1.6), market price P = 30.
P = MC ⇒ 30 = 4 + 2Q ⇒ Q = 13. TC = 100 + 52 + 169 = 321, TR = 390,
profit = **69**.

## Shutdown and the short-run supply curve

In the short run, fixed costs are paid anyway. The firm should produce if
revenue covers **variable** costs:

- P ≥ min AVC → produce where P = MC (it may still make a loss, but smaller
  than FC).
- P < min AVC → **shut down** (produce 0, lose only FC).

So the short-run supply curve of the firm is the MC curve **above min AVC**.
Market supply = horizontal sum of firms' supply curves.

*Worked example.* With TC = 100 + 4Q + Q², AVC = 4 + Q, min AVC = 4 (as Q→0).
At P = 20: Q = 8, TR = 160, TC = 100 + 32 + 64 = 196 → loss 36, but shutting
down would lose 100 → keep producing.

## Long run: entry, exit, zero profit

If firms earn **economic profit**, new firms enter, supply shifts right, the
price falls. If they make losses, firms exit, supply shifts left, the price
rises. In the long run:

```
P = MC = min ATC        and   economic profit = 0
```

Zero *economic* profit still means a normal return on the owners' resources
(implicit costs are included). Competition hands the gains from cost cuts to
consumers: the first firm with a new technology earns profits temporarily,
until rivals copy it.

## Efficiency of competition

- **Allocative efficiency**: P = MC — the value of the last unit to buyers
  equals its cost; total surplus is maximised (lesson 1.4).
- **Productive efficiency**: long-run output at min ATC.

## When "competition" means a few rivals

With **identical products** and buyers who always pick the cheapest seller,
even two firms can drive the price to marginal cost (Bertrand competition,
lesson 2.3). When products differ, buyers have imperfect information, or
firms face capacity limits, prices stay above MC. Lessons 1.8, 1.9 and 2.3
explore those cases.

## Contestable markets

Even a market with few firms can behave competitively if entry and exit are
free and fast (**contestable markets**): the threat of hit-and-run entry
disciplines prices.

## Summary

- Price taker: MR = P; produce where P = MC.
- Shut down if P < min AVC; short-run supply = MC above min AVC.
- Long run: free entry drives economic profit to zero, P = min ATC.
- Perfect competition is the efficiency benchmark.
