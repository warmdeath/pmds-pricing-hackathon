# 1.10 Market failures and information

## The big picture

Competitive markets maximise total surplus **if** certain conditions hold:
all costs and benefits are priced, goods are rival and excludable, and
information is symmetric. When they fail, markets can produce too much, too
little, or nothing at all. This lesson lists the main failures and the
standard remedies.

> **Analogy.** A market is an autopilot that flies well in clear weather. In
> fog (hidden information) or with an engine leaking fuel onto other planes
> (externalities), the autopilot still flies, just not to the right place.

## Externalities

An **externality** is a cost or benefit that falls on third parties and is
not reflected in the price.

- **Negative** (pollution, congestion): marginal social cost > private cost
  → the market overproduces.
- **Positive** (vaccination, R&D spillovers): social benefit > private
  benefit → underproduction.

Remedies: **Pigouvian tax** equal to the marginal external damage (makes the
polluter internalise it), subsidies for positive externalities, cap-and-trade
permits, regulation. **Coase theorem**: if property rights are clear and
bargaining is costless, private negotiation reaches efficiency whoever holds
the rights — in practice, transaction costs often prevent it.

## Public goods and common resources

Goods are classified by **rivalry** (my use reduces yours) and
**excludability** (you can prevent use):

| | excludable | non-excludable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rival | private goods (bread) | common resources (fish stocks) |
| non-rival | club goods (streaming) | public goods (lighthouse, defence) |

- Public goods suffer **free riding**: everyone hopes others pay.
- Common resources suffer the **tragedy of the commons**: each user ignores
  the depletion imposed on others → overuse. (Compare a shared, limited stock
  of inputs that many firms draw from.)

## Asymmetric information

- **Adverse selection** (hidden *type*, before the deal): Akerlof's "market
  for lemons" — buyers can't tell good used cars from bad, offer an average
  price, owners of good cars withdraw, quality falls, the market can unravel.
  Remedies: **signalling** (warranties, education), **screening** (insurance
  menus), reputation, certification.
- **Moral hazard** (hidden *action*, after the deal): insured drivers drive
  less carefully; managers take risks with shareholders' money. Remedies:
  deductibles, incentive pay, monitoring.
- **Principal-agent problems**: the agent acts for the principal but has
  different goals and more information.

## Market power as a failure

Monopoly and collusion (lessons 1.8, 2.3) also reduce surplus. Competition
authorities (the EU Commission, national antitrust agencies) prohibit cartels
and abuse of dominance and review mergers.

## Government failure

Interventions can fail too: poor information, lobbying (rent seeking),
unintended consequences (rent control → housing shortages). The relevant
comparison is imperfect market vs imperfect government.

## Information as an economic good

Information is costly to collect and **non-rival** once produced. Markets
reveal information through prices and transactions; what each participant can
observe shapes what they can infer. Public information (e.g. published
inventories or posted prices) can make markets more transparent; it can also
make coordination among sellers easier (lesson 2.4).

## Summary

- Externalities: price ≠ social cost → Pigouvian taxes, permits, Coase.
- Public goods: free riding; commons: overuse.
- Adverse selection (hidden type) vs moral hazard (hidden action).
- Market power and government failure complete the list.
