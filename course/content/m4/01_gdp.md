# 4.1 Measuring the economy: GDP, real vs nominal, growth

## The big picture

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole: total output, inflation,
unemployment, interest rates, trade. Its first task is **measurement** —
without a reliable thermometer you cannot tell whether the patient has a fever.
The thermometer of output is **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**.

> **Analogy.** GDP is the economy's total "sales receipt" for one year — but
> only for final products, so a car's tyres are not counted twice (once when
> sold to the car maker, again inside the car).

## Definition

**GDP** = market value of all **final** goods and services **produced within
a country** in a period.

- **Final** vs **intermediate**: only final goods count; intermediate inputs
  are already inside final prices. Equivalent: sum of **value added** at each
  stage.
- **Produced**, not sold: inventory investment counts (unsold goods produced
  this year add to GDP as investment in inventories).
- **Within the country** (domestic) — vs **GNI/GNP**, which counts income of
  residents wherever earned.
- Excludes second-hand sales, pure financial transactions, transfers, most
  household production and (officially) much of the shadow economy.

*Value-added example.* Farmer sells wheat €100 → miller sells flour €150 →
baker sells bread €250. Value added: 100 + 50 + 100 = **€250** = final sale.

## Three approaches

1. **Expenditure**: Y = C + I + G + NX (consumption, investment including
   inventories, government purchases, net exports X − M).
2. **Income**: wages + profits + rents + interest (+ indirect taxes,
   depreciation).
3. **Production**: sum of value added by sector.

All three give the same number (up to statistical discrepancy) because every
euro spent is someone's income.

*Worked example.* C = 1,100, I = 350, G = 400, X = 600, M = 550 →
GDP = 1,100 + 350 + 400 + 50 = **1,900**.

Italy's GDP is roughly €2 trillion; consumption about 60 % of it.

## Nominal vs real GDP

- **Nominal GDP**: current prices. Rises with output **or** prices.
- **Real GDP**: constant (base-year) prices — measures volume.
- **GDP deflator** = nominal / real × 100.

*Worked example.* Year 1: 10 pizzas @ €8, 5 books @ €20 → nominal 180.
Year 2: 12 pizzas @ €9, 6 books @ €21 → nominal 234. Real (year-1 prices):
12·8 + 6·20 = 216. Deflator = 234/216·100 = **108.3**; real growth =
216/180 − 1 = **20 %**; nominal growth 30 %.

## Growth rates

- Growth rate = (Y_t − Y_{t−1})/Y_{t−1}.
- **Compound annual growth rate** CAGR = (Y_end/Y_start)^(1/n) − 1.
- Small differences compound: 2 % vs 3 % growth over 35 years → output
  doubles vs nearly triples (rule of 70: doubling time ≈ 70/g).
- **GDP per capita** adjusts for population; **PPP** adjusts for price levels
  across countries.

## Limits of GDP

GDP ignores leisure, inequality, environmental damage, unpaid work and
quality improvements. Alternatives: HDI, "beyond GDP" dashboards. Still, GDP
per capita correlates strongly with health, education and life satisfaction.

## Summary

- GDP = value of final goods and services produced domestically = sum of
  value added.
- Y = C + I + G + NX; expenditure = income = production.
- Real GDP removes price changes; deflator = nominal/real × 100.
- Growth compounds; rule of 70.
