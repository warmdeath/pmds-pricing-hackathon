# 4.2 Money, inflation and interest rates

## The big picture

**Inflation** is a sustained rise in the general price level — a fall in what
money can buy. It redistributes wealth, distorts decisions and, when high and
volatile, damages growth. To understand it we need to understand **money**.

> **Analogy.** Money is a measuring stick for value. Inflation is a stick that
> shrinks a little every year: prices in centimetres keep growing although the
> things measured haven't changed.

## Money

Functions: **medium of exchange**, **unit of account**, **store of value**.
Today money is **fiat** (not backed by gold) and mostly bank deposits.

- **Monetary base** (M0): currency + bank reserves at the central bank.
- **M1**: currency + overnight deposits; **M2**, **M3** add less liquid
  deposits and instruments.
- Commercial banks **create money** when they lend (a loan creates a deposit);
  the central bank steers this through interest rates and regulation.

## Measuring inflation

- **CPI** (consumer price index): cost of a fixed basket of consumer goods;
  in the euro area the **HICP**. Inflation = % change in the index.
- **Core inflation** excludes volatile food and energy.
- **GDP deflator**: all domestically produced goods.
- CPI biases: substitution, quality changes, new goods → slight overstatement.

*Worked example.* Basket cost €200 in 2024 and €212 in 2025 → inflation
**6 %**. A salary rising from €30,000 to €31,200 (+4 %) is a **real** cut of
about 2 %: 31,200/1.06 = €29,434 in 2024 euros.

## Quantity theory of money

```
M · V = P · Y          ⇒   %ΔM + %ΔV ≈ %ΔP + %ΔY
```

With stable velocity V, in the long run inflation ≈ money growth − real
growth. Money growth 7 %, real growth 2 % → inflation ≈ **5 %**. Hyperinflations
(Weimar Germany, Zimbabwe, Venezuela) are always episodes of extreme money
printing, usually to finance deficits.

## Costs of inflation

- **Expected** inflation: shoe-leather costs (holding less cash), menu costs
  (repricing), tax distortions, relative-price confusion.
- **Unexpected** inflation: redistributes from lenders to borrowers and from
  people with fixed nominal incomes.
- **Deflation** is also costly: it raises the real burden of debt and can
  make people postpone spending (Japan's lost decades).
- Central banks aim at low, stable inflation — the ECB targets **2 %** over
  the medium term.

## Interest rates: nominal and real

**Fisher equation**:

```
(1 + i) = (1 + r)(1 + π)     ⇒    r ≈ i − π
```

i nominal rate, r real rate, π inflation (expected, for decisions).
Deposit at 3 % with 5 % inflation → real return ≈ **−2 %**.

## Causes of inflation in the short run

- **Demand-pull**: spending exceeds productive capacity (overheating).
- **Cost-push**: input prices rise (energy shock 2022, oil shocks 1970s).
- **Expectations**: if everyone expects prices to rise, wages and prices are
  set higher — inflation can become self-fulfilling; credibility of the
  central bank anchors expectations.
- **Wage-price spirals**: wages chase prices, prices chase wages.

## Summary

- Money: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value.
- Inflation = % change in a price index; real = nominal adjusted for prices.
- MV = PY: long-run inflation ≈ money growth − real growth.
- r ≈ i − π; the ECB targets 2 %.
